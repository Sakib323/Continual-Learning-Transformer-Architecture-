Evaluation design · CLMS

Signature Probes

You're right about training from scratch, and right that internal diagnostics beat a behavioural benchmark at this stage. Here's the correction to my earlier advice, and a probe suite built on one principle: every mechanism family claims a specific internal effect, so measure that effect directly rather than inferring it from accuracy.

Correction

From scratch is the right call

I was optimizing for the wrong thing

I pushed pretrained checkpoints because I was thinking about LLM realism — matching how models actually get updated in practice. That's the phase-5 concern, not the phase-3 one. For ranking mechanisms, a pretrained checkpoint is actively worse, for three reasons I should have led with:

  • Several mechanisms only exist if present at initialization. Memory layers, k-WTA sparsity, XdG gating, MoE routing, CMS frequency levels, sparse weight masks — these are architectural priors that shape what gets learned. Graft them onto a finished model and you measure grafting damage plus mechanism, not the mechanism.
  • A checkpoint has seen unknown data. When you measure forgetting on task 1, you can't tell whether the model already knew it. From scratch, you own the entire data history — which is the exact property I called decisive about OLMo 2's openness, and you get it for free by pretraining yourself.
  • The classic literature does it this way. EWC, SI, iCaRL, GPM, van de Ven's three scenarios, continual backprop — nearly all of it ran on small networks trained from scratch. van de Ven used MLPs on MNIST. Dohare used small ConvNets over 800 tasks. They weren't compromising; small-from-scratch is the correct instrument for this question.

So: initialize from OLMo 2 source, inject mechanisms at construction, train from random weights. That was the right plan.

But go much smaller than 0.5B. The real unlock here is scaling down, not up. You don't need a good language model to rank continual-learning mechanisms — you need one that demonstrably learns tasks and demonstrably forgets them. That happens at 20–50M parameters, where a full task sequence trains in minutes and the whole 50-run sweep fits in an afternoon of rented time. 0.5B for the ranking sweep would buy you nothing except a slower answer.

The size ladder

~20–50MRanking sweep OLMo 2 arch, small config, from scratch. Phases 1–4. Minutes per run, so 3 seeds × 15 mechanisms is trivial. This is where you learn which mechanisms work.
~150MRank confirmation Does the small-scale ordering hold? Run the top 5 plus controls. Where the ranking shifts, that shift is itself a result worth reporting.
~0.5BYour target The winning stack only. Real pretraining budget, real language modelling, the model you actually wanted to build.
1B+External validity TRACE and the LLM capability deltas come back here — general ability, instruction following, safety. This is where behavioural benchmarks earn their cost.

Same arch code at every rung. Moving up is a config change, because mechanisms plug in through the contract rather than through the model.

The organizing principle

Every family has a signature

Accuracy tells you that forgetting happened. It never tells you why, and two mechanisms can reach identical accuracy by completely different internal routes — which means an accuracy-only comparison can't tell you what to combine, what to fix, or what's silently broken.

But each mechanism family makes a specific, falsifiable claim about the network's internals. Sparsity claims low activation overlap. Localization claims concentrated updates. Gradient projection claims orthogonal subspaces. Plasticity maintenance claims preserved rank. Merging claims low loss barriers. Measure the claimed quantity directly, and you learn whether the mechanism is doing what its paper says — independently of whether that helps.

This also solves the invisible-failure problem properly. A self-test proves a mechanism ran. A signature probe proves it did the thing it claims. If EWC is enabled and displacement on high-Fisher parameters doesn't drop, EWC is broken — and you know that immediately, regardless of what accuracy says. Make the signature check an automatic assertion in every run, not something you look at afterwards.
The suite

Seventeen probes, four groups

GROUP A

Representation — what the network encodes

5 probes
A1

Effective rank of hidden activations

Collapse of representational dimensionality is the clearest signature of plasticity loss. Compute per layer on a fixed probe batch, tracked over the whole stream.

H = activations (n × d) ;  σ = singular values of H
p_i = σ_i / Σσ_j
eff_rank = exp( −Σ p_i log p_i )      entropy of the spectrum
A2

Representation drift (linear CKA)

How far did the representation of old data move while learning new tasks? Evaluate on task-1 inputs, comparing activations before and after task k. This is the mechanistic core of forgetting.

CKA(X,Y) = ‖YᵀX‖²_F / ( ‖XᵀX‖_F · ‖YᵀY‖_F )
1.0 = unchanged, 0 = unrelated. Report per layer — drift is rarely uniform.
A3

Cross-task representation overlap

CKA between task-a and task-b activations. High overlap predicts interference; it's the quantity that decides whether sharing helps or hurts on this particular task sequence.

A4

Activation overlap (Jaccard)

The direct test of the sparsity argument. Fraction of units co-active across two tasks, over units active on either.

act_a = { i : mean|h_i| on task a > τ }
overlap = |act_a ∩ act_b| / |act_a ∪ act_b|
sparsity mechanisms must drive this down; if they don't, they aren't working
A5

Dead and dormant unit fraction

Units whose activation is ~0 across the probe set. One of the three correlates of plasticity loss, and cheap enough to log every few hundred steps.

GROUP B

Parameters — what actually moved

4 probes
B1

Displacement map

‖θ_after − θ_before‖ per layer and per parameter group, after each task. Cross-reference against Fisher importance to test the regularization family directly, and split by parameter group to test the CLS claim that fast and slow components update at genuinely different rates.

B2

Update concentration

The measurable form of "localized updates." What fraction of parameters accounts for 90% of total displacement? This is the quantity the sparse-memory result is really about.

d = sort( |Δθ| , descending )
conc90 = min{ k : Σ_{i≤k} d_i ≥ 0.9 · Σ d } / |θ|
full finetuning ≈ 0.5+ ; sparse mechanisms should reach 1e-3 or below
B3

Weight magnitude growth

Mean |θ| per layer over the stream. The third plasticity correlate — and an early warning that fires long before accuracy degrades.

B4

Layer-wise forgetting attribution

Which layers actually cause the forgetting? After learning task k, restore one layer at a time to its task-1 values and re-measure task-1 accuracy. The layer whose restoration recovers the most is where forgetting lives — and that tells you where to put a mechanism, not just whether it worked.

GROUP C

Gradients & geometry — why it moved

4 probes
C1

Gradient interference

Cosine between old-task and new-task gradients at the current parameters. Negative means direct conflict — every step on the new task actively damages the old one. This is the quantity GEM, A-GEM and MER all target, so measuring it tells you whether they're earning their cost.

interference = ⟨g_old , g_new⟩ / (‖g_old‖ · ‖g_new‖)
C2

Gradient subspace angles

GPM's entire premise, made measurable. Take the top-k gradient subspaces for two tasks, compute principal angles; near-90° means the tasks occupy separable directions and projection methods should work well here.

U_a, U_b = top-k left singular vectors of each task's gradient matrix
angles = arccos( singular values of U_aᵀ U_b )
C3

Sharpness

Trace of the empirical Fisher, plus loss increase under Gaussian weight perturbation at several scales. A cheap forward-looking predictor: flatter solutions after task k tend to forget less at task k+1.

C4

Loss barrier between task solutions

Interpolate between the task-a and task-b parameter vectors and measure the loss bump. A low barrier means the solutions share a basin — which predicts that merging would have worked, and tells you something real about whether the tasks conflict at all.

barrier = max_{α∈[0,1]} L(αθ_a + (1−α)θ_b) − [αL(θ_a) + (1−α)L(θ_b)]
GROUP D

Behaviour — the outer layer

4 probes
D1

Full accuracy matrix

A[i][j] = accuracy on task j after training through task i. Every retention metric — AA, AIA, FM, BWT, FWT, intransigence — derives from this one object. Evaluating only the current task is the most common way to accidentally measure nothing.

D2

Logit drift

KL divergence of outputs on a frozen probe set, before versus after each task. Catches functional change that accuracy hides — a model can stay correct while its confidence structure is destroyed, and that degradation shows up as accuracy loss one task later.

D3

Per-example forgetting events

Which specific examples flip correct → incorrect, and do they cluster? Clustered forgetting means a coherent capability was lost; diffuse forgetting means general degradation. Different problems, different fixes, invisible in aggregate accuracy.

D4

Three-scenario evaluation

Run the same stream under Task-IL, Domain-IL and Class-IL. Methods that look strong with task identity provided routinely collapse without it — that gap is one of the most informative single numbers you can produce, and it costs only evaluation time.

The stream

What to train on instead of TRACE

Skipping TRACE for now is right — it was built to challenge aligned 7B+ models, and at 20–50M its tasks would sit near chance on both sides of training. You need a task sequence with a large, reliable floor–ceiling gap at small scale.

Synthetic algorithmic tasks are the strongest fit, and they're what makes the internal probes interpretable:

  • Exact ground truth — accuracy is unambiguous, no metric ambiguity to argue about
  • Tunable overlap — you can dial how much two tasks share, which turns A3/A4 into a controlled experiment instead of an observation
  • Minutes to train — the whole reason the sweep is affordable
  • Known internal structure — when a probe moves you can often say what it means, which is rarely true on natural text

A reasonable sequence: modular arithmetic at several moduli, copy, reverse, sort, key–value recall, induction. Then a second, more realistic sequence — split language modelling over disjoint domains of a small corpus — as the bridge toward TRACE.

Build the fixed probe set first. A frozen, never-trained-on batch from every task, held constant for the entire project. Almost every probe above is defined relative to it, and if it changes mid-project the numbers stop being comparable across runs.
